A ring signature is a type of digital signature that allows a member of a group to sign a message on behalf of the group,
while keeping their exact identity anonymous to anyone verifying the signature.

### Core Properties

- **Anonymity:** An outside observer or verifier can confirm that the signature was created by _someone_ in the designated
  group of public keys, but it is computationally infeasible to determine which specific individual generated it.
- **No Setup Coordinator:** Unlike traditional group signatures, ring signatures require no trusted coordinator, group
  manager, or complex setup phase. A signer can spontaneously choose any set of other users' public keys to form a "ring"
  along with their own private key.
- **Unforgeability:** Only a legitimate holder of at least one of the private keys corresponding to the public keys in the
  ring can successfully generate a valid signature.

### How It Works (Conceptually)

When a signer wants to create a ring signature, they combine their own private key with the public keys of several other
participants. Using a cryptographic combining function, the algorithm links all the keys together into a circular structure.
Anyone can verify the signature using the public keys of the entire ring, ensuring authenticity without exposing the signer's
identity.

### Common Applications

- **Privacy-Centric Cryptocurrencies:** Cryptocurrencies like Monero use variations of ring signatures to obscure the sender
  of a transaction, ensuring financial privacy on a public blockchain.
- **Whistleblowing and Anonymous Publishing:** Allowing individuals to sign documents or leaks proving they belong to a
  specific organization without revealing their personal identity, protecting them from retaliation.
- **Anonymous Voting:** Enabling secure, verifiable voting where participants can prove their vote is valid and cast by an
  eligible member without linking the ballot back to them.
