Common symmetric encryption algorithms include AES, ChaCha20, Blowfish, and legacy standards like DES and Triple DES.

- **AES (Advanced Encryption Standard):** The global industry standard for secure data encryption. It operates on 128-bit
  blocks and supports key lengths of 128, 192, and 256 bits. It is widely used across HTTPS, Wi-Fi security (WPA3), file
  encryption, and messaging apps.
- **ChaCha20:** A high-speed stream cipher designed by Daniel J. Bernstein as a secure and efficient alternative to AES,
  particularly on mobile devices, ARM processors, and hardware without dedicated AES instruction sets. It is commonly paired
  with the Poly1305 authenticator.
- **Blowfish:** A symmetric block cipher created by Bruce Schneier featuring a 64-bit block size and a variable key length
  from 32 to 448 bits. While secure, it has largely been superseded by modern alternatives due to its small block size.
- **Twofish:** The successor to Blowfish, also designed by Bruce Schneier. It is a 128-bit block cipher supporting key sizes
  up to 256 bits and was one of the five finalists in the AES selection process.
- **Triple DES (3DES):** A legacy algorithm that applies the older Data Encryption Standard (DES) cipher three times
  consecutively to each data block. It is deprecated and being phased out globally due to small block size vulnerabilities
  and slow performance.
- **RC4:** A historical stream cipher once heavily used in SSL, TLS, and WEP. It is cryptographically broken and considered
  insecure for modern applications.
