## Overview of Information Theory

**Information Theory** is a mathematical branch of computer science and electrical engineering that deals with quantifying,
storing, and communicating data.

Pioneered by mathematician **Claude Shannon** in 1948, it forms the theoretical backbone of modern digital communication, the
internet, data compression, and even cryptography.

---

### Core Concepts

- **Entropy (Uncertainty):** In information theory, entropy measures the amount of unpredictability or "surprise" in a set of
  data. If an event is completely predictable, its entropy is zero. If it is completely random, its entropy is high.
- **Data Compression:** Shannon proved that redundant data can be squeezed out without losing meaning, leading to the
  foundations of how we compress files (like ZIP, JPEG, or MP3).
- **Channel Capacity:** This defines the maximum rate at which data can be reliably transmitted over a communication channel
  (like a Wi-Fi connection or fiber-optic cable) in the presence of noise.
- **Error Correction:** Because physical communication channels (like space probes talking to Earth) experience interference,
  information theory uses mathematical redundancy (like parity bits or Hamming codes) to detect and fix errors automatically.

---

### Why It Matters Today

Without information theory, our modern digital world wouldn't exist. It dictates how efficiently we can stream
high-definition video on YouTube, send messages across cellular networks, and store massive amounts of data on solid-state
drives.
