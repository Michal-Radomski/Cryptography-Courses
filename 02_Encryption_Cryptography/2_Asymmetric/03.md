RSA is an asymmetric cryptographic algorithm used widely to securely transmit data over the internet, relying on the
mathematical difficulty of factoring the product of two large prime numbers.

The algorithm operates through three main phases: key generation, encryption, and decryption.

### 1. Key Generation

- Choose two distinct large prime numbers, $p$ and $q$.
- Compute their product $n = pq$, which is used as the modulus for both the public and private keys. The length of $n$ in
  bits is the key length.
- Compute Euler's totient function: $\phi(n) = (p-1)(q-1)$.
- Choose an integer $e$ (the public exponent) such that $1 < e < \phi(n)$ and $\text{gcd}(e, \phi(n)) = 1$ (typically
  $65537$).
- Compute $d$ (the private exponent) as the modular multiplicative inverse of $e$ modulo $\phi(n)$:

$$d \equiv e^{-1} \pmod{\phi(n)}$$

- The **public key** consists of the modulus $n$ and the public exponent $e$. The **private key** consists of the modulus $n$
  and the private exponent $d$.

### 2. Encryption

Given a plaintext message $m$ represented as an integer less than $n$, the sender encrypts it into ciphertext $c$ using the
recipient's public key $(n, e)$:

$$c \equiv m^e \pmod n$$

### 3. Decryption

The recipient recovers the original plaintext message $m$ from the ciphertext $c$ using their private key $(n, d)$:

$$m \equiv c^d \pmod n$$

The security of RSA rests on the integer factorization problem: while it is computationally simple to multiply two large
primes together to get $n$, factoring $n$ back into $p$ and $q$ is practically impossible for sufficiently large keys (such
as 2048 or 4096 bits) using classical computers.
