The discrete logarithm problem is a mathematical challenge of finding the integer exponent $x$ given a base $g$ and a value
$h$ such that $g^x \equiv h \pmod p$, where $p$ is a prime number and the operations are performed within a finite cyclic
group.

While calculating $g^x \pmod p$ (modular exponentiation) is computationally efficient even for very large numbers, reversing
the process—finding $x$ when given $g$, $h$, and $p$—is extremely difficult for large inputs because no efficient classical
algorithm is known to solve it quickly.

This asymmetry forms the foundational security for many widely used public-key cryptography protocols, including:

- **Diffie-Hellman Key Exchange:** Allows two parties to establish a shared secret over an insecure channel.
- **Digital Signature Algorithm (DSA):** Used for digital signatures and authentication.
- **Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC):** A variant of public-key cryptography based on the algebraic structure of elliptic
  curves over finite fields, where the discrete logarithm problem is similarly difficult but allows for smaller key sizes
  with equivalent security.
