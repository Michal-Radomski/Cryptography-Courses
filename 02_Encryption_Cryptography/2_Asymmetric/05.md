Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) is a comprehensive framework of hardware, software, policies, and procedures used to create,
manage, distribute, use, store, and revoke digital certificates and secure electronic transactions.

### Core Components

- **Certificate Authority (CA):** A trusted entity that issues digital certificates, verifying that a public key belongs to a
  specific individual, organization, or device.
- **Registration Authority (RA):** An entity that acts as an extension of the CA, responsible for verifying the identity of
  certificate applicants before the CA issues the certificate.
- **Digital Certificates:** Electronic credentials (commonly following the X.509 standard) that securely bind a public key to
  an identity, including metadata and the issuing CA's digital signature.
- **Repository:** A secure database or directory used for storing and distributing digital certificates and Certificate
  Revocation Lists (CRLs).
- **Validation Authority:** A system used to verify the status of a certificate in real-time, often via the Online
  Certificate Status Protocol (OCSP).

### Primary Functions and Use Cases

- **Authentication:** Verifying the identity of users, servers, or devices, such as confirming a browser is communicating
  with a legitimate website via HTTPS/TLS.
- **Confidentiality:** Facilitating the exchange of keys used for symmetric encryption to protect data in transit.
- **Integrity:** Using digital signatures to ensure that transmitted data has not been intercepted and altered.
- **Non-Repudiation:** Ensuring that a sender cannot successfully deny having sent a digitally signed message or document.
