Common asymmetric encryption algorithms include RSA, Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC), Diffie-Hellman, and modern
post-quantum standards.

- **RSA (Rivest-Shamir-Adleman):** The most widely deployed asymmetric algorithm, relying on the extreme mathematical
  difficulty of factoring the product of two large prime numbers. It is used for digital signatures, secure key exchange, and
  legacy HTTPS certificates.
- **ECC (Elliptic Curve Cryptography):** A modern alternative to RSA that achieves equivalent cryptographic security with
  much smaller key sizes, making it significantly faster and more efficient for mobile devices, TLS 1.3, and
  cryptocurrencies. Common variants include ECDSA for digital signatures and ECDH for key exchanges.
- **Diffie-Hellman (DH):** A foundational cryptographic protocol that allows two parties to establish a shared secret over an
  insecure communications channel without ever transmitting the secret itself.
- **Ed25519 and X25519:** High-performance elliptic curve algorithms designed for speed and strong security guarantees. They
  are widely used in modern secure messaging protocols, SSH keys, and modern TLS implementations.
- **Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) Algorithms:** Newer standards (such as ML-KEM, based on CRYSTALS-Kyber) selected by NIST
  to resist future attacks from quantum computers, which are currently being integrated into long-term security architectures
  to replace vulnerable traditional algorithms like RSA.
