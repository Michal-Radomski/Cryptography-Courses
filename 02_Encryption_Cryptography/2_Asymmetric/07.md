TLS 1.3 exclusively uses **Diffie-Hellman-based key exchange** algorithms, specifically Ephemeral Diffie-Hellman (DHE) and
Elliptic Curve Diffie-Hellman (ECDH).

Unlike older versions of TLS, TLS 1.3 dropped support for static RSA key exchanges because they do not provide Perfect
Forward Secrecy (PFS). Every key exchange in TLS 1.3 must be ephemeral, ensuring that even if a server's long-term private
key is compromised in the future, past session traffic cannot be decrypted.

Commonly supported groups and curves during a TLS 1.3 handshake include:

- **X25519** and **X448** (Elliptic curves)
- **Secp256r1** and **Secp384r1** (NIST elliptic curves)
- **Finite-field Diffie-Hellman (FFDHE)** parameters (for traditional prime-modulus DH)

Additionally, modern implementations of TLS 1.3 increasingly support **hybrid post-quantum key exchanges** (such as
combinations of X25519 and post-quantum algorithms like ML-KEM) to protect data against future quantum computing threats.
