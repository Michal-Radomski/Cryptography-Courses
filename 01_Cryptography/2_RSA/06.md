## Comparing RSA and Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC)

While **RSA** has been a gold standard for decades, modern cryptography has increasingly shifted toward **Elliptic Curve
Cryptography (ECC)**. Both are asymmetric algorithms, but they use completely different mathematical foundations.

---

### Key Differences at a Glance

- **Mathematical Foundation:**
- _RSA:_ Relies on the difficulty of factoring the product of two large prime numbers.
- _ECC:_ Relies on the algebraic structure of elliptic curves over finite fields (specifically, the elliptic curve discrete
  logarithm problem).

- **Key Size and Efficiency:**
- _RSA:_ Requires very large keys (typically 2048 or 4096 bits) to achieve high security.
- _ECC:_ Provides the same level of security with drastically smaller keys (e.g., a 256-bit ECC key offers comparable
  security to a 3048-bit RSA key).

- **Performance:**
- _RSA:_ Slower key generation, encryption, and signature verification, especially with larger keys.
- _ECC:_ Much faster performance with lower CPU, memory, and bandwidth consumption, making it ideal for mobile devices, IoT,
  and modern web traffic (like TLS 1.3).

---

### Why ECC is the Modern Choice

Because of its efficiency, ECC is heavily used in modern applications like smartphone operating systems, secure messaging
apps (such as Signal and WhatsApp), and cryptocurrencies. It offers high-grade security without draining device batteries or
slowing down network connections.
