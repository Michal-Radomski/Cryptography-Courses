## Understanding the AES Key Expansion Module

The **Key Expansion** module is a fundamental part of the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) algorithm. While the main
encryption and decryption rounds use a single initial secret key, AES actually requires a separate **round key** for each
individual round of processing, plus one for the initial addition step.

The Key Expansion module takes the original cipher key (the input key) and expands it into a larger schedule of round keys.

---

### Key Sizes and Expansion Requirements

Depending on the AES variant being used, the initial key length and the required expanded key size differ:

- **AES-128:** Uses a 128-bit key (16 bytes) and expands it into **11 total round keys** (176 bytes total).
- **AES-192:** Uses a 192-bit key (24 bytes) and expands it into **13 total round keys** (208 bytes total).
- **AES-256:** Uses a 256-bit key (32 bytes) and expands it into **15 total round keys** (240 bytes total).

---

### Core Operations in Key Expansion

The expansion process takes the words of the previous keys to generate new words using three primary cryptographic
operations:

- **RotWord (Rotate Word):** A cyclic permutation that shifts the 4 bytes of a 32-bit word to the left by one byte position
  (e.g., $[B_0, B_1, B_2, B_3]$ becomes $[B_1, B_2, B_3, B_0]$).
- **SubWord (Substitute Word):** A non-linear byte substitution step that passes each of the 4 bytes through the AES
  **S-Box** (Substitution Box) to provide confusion and break linearity.
- **Rcon (Round Constant XOR):** An XOR operation combining the substituted word with a predefined round constant vector
  ($Rcon$). This prevents symmetry and structural weaknesses in the generated keys across different rounds.

---

### Why is Key Expansion Important?

- **Diffusion and Confusion:** It ensures that every bit of the expanded round keys depends heavily on the original secret
  key, making it computationally infeasible for attackers to work backward.
- **Security against Related-Key Attacks:** By incorporating round constants and non-linear transformations, it prevents
  structural correlations between keys used in different rounds.
