## What is Cryptology?

**Cryptology** is the overarching scientific study of secure communication. It combines mathematics, computer science, and
engineering to protect information from unauthorized access or tampering.

Cryptology is actually split into two main branches:

- **Cryptography:** The practice of _creating_ secure codes and encryption methods to protect data.
- **Cryptanalysis:** The practice of _analyzing_ and breaking those codes to find weaknesses (often called "hacking" or
  code-breaking).

---

## What is Cryptography?

**Cryptography** focuses specifically on transforming readable data (plaintext) into an unreadable format (ciphertext) so
only authorized parties can read it.

### Key Concepts:

- **Encryption:** The process of scrambling data using an algorithm and a "key" so it looks like random gibberish.
- **Decryption:** The reverse process of using a key to turn the scrambled ciphertext back into readable plaintext.
- **Keys:** Think of a cryptographic key like a physical password or combination lock. Without the correct key, unlocking the
  data is mathematically nearly impossible.

---

## Modern Examples in Everyday Life

You use cryptography every single day without even realizing it! Here are a few common places it shows up:

- **HTTPS & Web Browsing:** The padlock icon in your browser URL bar means your connection to a website is encrypted using
  protocols like TLS (Transport Layer Security).
- **Messaging Apps:** Apps like WhatsApp or Signal use **end-to-end encryption**, meaning only you and the person you are
  messaging can read the texts—not even the app company can see them.
- **Passwords & Accounts:** When you create an account, websites don't store your actual password; they store a cryptographic
  "hash" (a secure mathematical fingerprint) of it.
