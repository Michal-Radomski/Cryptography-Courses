On average, mining a single Bitcoin requires hundreds of thousands of kilowatt-hours (kWh) of electricity—with estimates
typically ranging between **500,000 and over 700,000 kWh**, depending on the efficiency of the mining hardware used. To put
that into perspective, 700,000 kWh is roughly equivalent to the total electricity consumed by an average U.S. household over
50 to 60 years.

The exact amount of energy changes constantly based on a few key factors:

- **Network Difficulty:** Bitcoin's code automatically adjusts how hard the cryptographic puzzles are to solve every two
  weeks. As more miners join the network and use more powerful equipment, the difficulty increases, requiring more energy to
  mine each coin.
- **Hardware Efficiency:** Modern mining is done using specialized computers called ASICs (Application-Specific Integrated
  Circuits). Newer models are significantly more energy-efficient than older generations, meaning miners using
  state-of-the-art equipment use less electricity per coin than those using older hardware.
- **Global Hashrate:** This measures the total combined computational power being used by all miners on the network at any
  given time.
