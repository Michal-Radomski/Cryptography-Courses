**Proof-of-Work (PoW) Consensus**

- **Definition:** Proof-of-Work is a cryptographic consensus mechanism used by decentralized blockchain networks—most notably
  Bitcoin—to validate transactions and secure the network against malicious interference.
- **The Mining Process:** Participants known as "miners" compete to solve computationally heavy mathematical puzzles. These
  puzzles require brute-forcing cryptographic hash functions (like SHA-256) until a hash matching a specific target
  difficulty is found.
- **Network Security:** Because solving these puzzles demands vast amounts of electricity and specialized computer hardware
  (ASICs), it creates a high financial barrier to entry. An attacker would need to control over 50% of the network's total
  computing power to successfully rewrite transaction history, making large-scale fraud economically unviable.
- **Block Addition:** The first miner to solve the puzzle earns the right to add the next block of verified transactions to
  the blockchain. As a reward, they receive newly minted cryptocurrency and transaction fees.
- **Drawbacks:** The primary criticism of Proof-of-Work is its massive energy consumption, which has led many newer
  blockchain networks to adopt alternative models like Proof-of-Stake (PoS), which require significantly less electricity.
