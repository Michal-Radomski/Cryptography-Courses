Cryptography is the foundational technology that secures cryptocurrencies, ensuring that transactions are safe, identities
are protected, and the digital ledger cannot be tampered with.

**Public-Key Cryptography (Wallets and Ownership)**

- **Key Pairs:** Every cryptocurrency user has a public key (similar to an account number or email address) and a private key
  (similar to a secret password).
- **Digital Signatures:** When you send cryptocurrency, you use your private key to sign the transaction. This creates a
  mathematical signature that anyone on the network can verify using your public key, proving you own the funds without
  revealing your secret key.

**Cryptographic Hash Functions (Data Integrity and Immutability)**

- **One-Way Functions:** Hash functions (like Bitcoin's SHA-256) take an input of any size and convert it into a fixed-length
  string of characters (a hash). It is mathematically impossible to reverse this process to find the original input.
- **Block Linking:** Each block in a blockchain contains the cryptographic hash of the previous block. If anyone tries to
  alter a past transaction, its hash changes, breaking the cryptographic chain and alerting the entire network to the
  tampering.

**Proof-of-Work (Network Consensus)**

- **Mining Puzzles:** In networks like Bitcoin, miners compete to solve complex cryptographic puzzles that require massive
  amounts of computational power.
- **Security:** This process, known as Proof-of-Work, makes it economically and computationally prohibitive for bad actors to
  rewrite transaction history or disrupt the network.

**Advanced Privacy (Zero-Knowledge Proofs)**

- **Enhanced Confidentiality:** Modern cryptographic tools like zero-knowledge proofs allow one party to prove to another
  that a statement is true (e.g., "this person has enough money to make this purchase") without revealing any specific
  details, such as account balances or identities.
