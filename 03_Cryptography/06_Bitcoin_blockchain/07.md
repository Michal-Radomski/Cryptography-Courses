An ASIC (Application-Specific Integrated Circuit) Bitcoin miner looks like a heavy-duty, industrial electronic box rather
than a standard desktop computer.

- **The Outer Shell:** It is typically housed in an oblong rectangular metal chassis (often made of aluminum or steel). Most
  models look like a thick, compact brick or a small server box.
- **The Cooling Fans:** On both ends of the device, you will usually see two large, high-speed computer fans (often sized
  around 120mm). Because they generate immense heat, these fans are built right into the front and back to pull a massive
  volume of air straight through the machine.
- **Cables and Connections:** On the front panel, there is an Ethernet port (RJ45) to connect to the internet, status
  indicator lights, a reset button, and slots where heavy-duty power cables plug in.
- **Inside the Box:** Though you can't see them without opening the metal case, the interior contains a control board (the
  "brain") and multiple **hashboards** covered in tiny custom microchips and aluminum heatsinks.

Because they are packed with industrial fans running at maximum speed, real ASIC miners are extremely loud—sounding similar
to a vacuum cleaner or a jet engine—and are rarely kept in normal living spaces for that reason.
