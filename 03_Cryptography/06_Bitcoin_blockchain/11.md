Double spending is the fraudulent attempt to spend the same digital currency twice, a fundamental problem with digital money
that Bitcoin solved. Unlike physical cash, which you physically hand over so you no longer possess it, digital files can
theoretically be copied and sent to two different people simultaneously.

Bitcoin prevents double spending through its decentralized public ledger, known as the **blockchain**, and a network of
computers (miners) that verify transactions.

Here is how the prevention works in practice:

- **Transaction Broadcasting:** When you send Bitcoin to someone, the transaction is broadcasted to the network, but it isn't
  final immediately.
- **Mempool and Validation:** Unconfirmed transactions sit in a holding area called the mempool. Miners collect these
  transactions and bundle them into a block.
- **The Consensus Check:** Before adding a block to the blockchain, the network checks to ensure the sender actually owns the
  funds and hasn't tried to spend them already. If a second transaction tries to spend the same coins, the network rejects it
  as invalid.
- **Confirmations:** Once a block is mined and added to the chain, the transaction receives one "confirmation." Each
  subsequent block added on top adds another confirmation, making the transaction practically irreversible and permanently
  stopping any double-spend attempt.
