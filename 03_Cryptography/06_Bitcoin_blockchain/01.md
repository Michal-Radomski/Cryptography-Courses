**Blockchain**

- **Decentralized Ledger:** Instead of being stored in a single central database controlled by an institution like a bank, a
  blockchain is a distributed digital ledger shared across a vast network of independent computers (nodes).
- **Blocks and Chains:** Transactions are grouped together into data packets called "blocks." Once a block reaches capacity,
  it is permanently locked and cryptographically chained to the previous block, creating a chronological and unalterable
  timeline.
- **Immutability:** Once information is written to a blockchain, it cannot be changed or deleted. Altering past data would
  require rewriting every subsequent block and controlling a majority of the network's computing power, making tampering
  virtually impossible.
- **Trustless Verification:** Because the system relies on cryptographic math and network consensus rather than a trusted
  middleman, participants can securely transact with strangers across the globe.

**Bitcoin**

- **First Cryptocurrency:** Launched in 2009 by an anonymous programmer (or group) operating under the pseudonym Satoshi
  Nakamoto, Bitcoin is the pioneer and largest digital currency in the world.
- **Decentralized Currency:** Bitcoin operates entirely on a peer-to-peer network without requiring a central bank,
  government, or payment processor to authorize transactions.
- **Finite Supply:** Unlike government-issued fiat currencies (like the US dollar or euro), which can be printed endlessly,
  Bitcoin has a hard-coded maximum limit of 21 million coins, giving it a scarce, deflationary nature similar to gold.
- **Mining and Consensus:** Bitcoin uses a consensus mechanism called Proof of Work. Miners use specialized computer hardware
  to solve complex mathematical puzzles, securing the network and validating transactions in exchange for newly minted
  bitcoin.
- **Store of Value:** While originally envisioned as a digital cash system for buying everyday goods, Bitcoin is most
  commonly used today as a long-term investment asset and a hedge against inflation due to its market volatility.
