The Extended Euclidean algorithm is an extension of the standard Euclidean algorithm that not only finds the greatest common
divisor (GCD) of two integers $a$ and $b$, but also finds the integers $x$ and $y$ (known as Bézout coefficients) that
satisfy Bézout's identity:

$$ax + by = \gcd(a, b)$$

This algorithm is critically important in number theory and cryptography—specifically for finding the modular multiplicative
inverse, which is required to calculate the private exponent $d$ in the RSA encryption algorithm.

---

### How It Works

The algorithm consists of two main phases:

1. **The Forward Pass (Division Steps):** Just like the standard Euclidean algorithm, you repeatedly apply division with
   remainder until the remainder is 0.
2. **The Backward Pass (Back Substitution):** You work backward through the equations to express the GCD as a linear
   combination of the original numbers $a$ and $b$.

---

### Step-by-Step Example

Let's find $\gcd(35, 15)$ and solve for $x$ and $y$ such that:

$$35x + 15y = \gcd(35, 15)$$

#### Step 1: Forward Pass (Find the GCD)

Apply division with remainder:

1. $35 = 2 \times 15 + 5$ _(Remainder is 5)_
2. $15 = 3 \times 5 + 0$ _(Remainder is 0, so the GCD is 5)_

#### Step 2: Backward Pass (Find the Coefficients)

Rearrange equation (1) to isolate the remainder ($5$):

$$5 = 35 - (2 \times 15)$$

We can rewrite this to match the form $35x + 15y = 5$:

$$35(1) + 15(-2) = 5$$

Thus, our coefficients are:

- $x = 1$
- $y = -2$
- $\gcd(35, 15) = 5$

---

### Application in RSA

In RSA, you need to find the private exponent $d$ such that:

$$d \cdot e \equiv 1 \pmod{\phi(n)}$$

This congruence can be rewritten as a linear Diophantine equation:

$$d \cdot e + k \cdot \phi(n) = 1$$

Since $e$ and $\phi(n)$ are coprime, their GCD is 1. Running the Extended Euclidean algorithm with inputs $e$ and $\phi(n)$
directly yields the coefficient $d$, serving as your private key.
