Here are the mathematical steps involved in creating and verifying a digital signature using the **RSA** algorithm.

Before diving into signing and verifying, remember that RSA relies on a public-private key pair generated from two large
prime numbers, $p$ and $q$, where the modulus $n = p \times q$ and the private exponent is $d$ (with public exponent $e$).

---

## 1. The Signing Process (Performed by the Sender)

When a sender wants to sign a message using their private key, they follow these steps:

- **Step 1: Hash the Message:** To handle large documents efficiently, the message $M$ is passed through a cryptographic hash
  function (like SHA-256) to produce a fixed-size message digest, let's call it $h$.
- **Step 2: Apply the Private Key:** The sender raises the hash value $h$ to the power of their private exponent $d$, modulo
  $n$.
- **Mathematical Formula:**

$$s = h^d \pmod n$$

- **Step 3: Output:** The resulting value $s$ is the digital signature, which is then sent along with the original message
  $M$.

---

## 2. The Verification Process (Performed by the Recipient)

When the recipient receives the message $M$ and the signature $s$, they use the sender's public key ($e$ and $n$) to verify
authenticity:

- **Step 1: Hash the Received Message:** The recipient applies the exact same hash function to the received message $M$ to
  compute the local hash digest $h$.
- **Step 2: Decrypt the Signature:** The recipient raises the signature $s$ to the power of the sender's public exponent $e$,
  modulo $n$.
- **Mathematical Formula:**

$$h' = s^e \pmod n$$

- **Step 3: Compare and Validate:** The recipient compares the newly computed hash $h$ with the decrypted signature $h'$. If
  $h = h'$, the signature is valid, proving the message came from the holder of the private key and was not altered in
  transit.
