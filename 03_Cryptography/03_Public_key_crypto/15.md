Yes, **contactless cards** (cards you tap to pay, as well as phones using Apple Pay or Google Pay) are **very
safe**—significantly safer than traditional magnetic stripe cards.

They use advanced security measures to protect your money and personal information:

### Key Security Features

- **Dynamic Cryptograms:** Just like chip-and-PIN cards, every contactless transaction creates a unique, one-time digital
  signature. Even if someone intercepted the radio signal, that specific data could never be used again.
- **Transaction Limits:** For larger purchases, terminals automatically require you to enter your PIN or use biometric
  verification (like Face ID or a fingerprint), preventing a thief from making massive purchases if your card is lost.
- **Short Range:** Contactless technology (NFC) only works when the card is within a couple of inches of the payment
  terminal. It is nearly impossible for someone to "walk past you" and steal money from your pocket.
- **Tokenization (Mobile Wallets):** If you use your phone or smartwatch to pay, the merchant never actually sees your real
  card number. Instead, they receive a temporary digital token.

### Potential Risks and How to Avoid Them

While the technology itself is secure, physical loss remains a risk:

- **Lost or Stolen Cards:** If someone steals your physical contactless card, they might be able to make a few small
  purchases below the PIN limit before you notice and block it.
- **How to stay safe:** Monitor your account alerts, freeze or lock your card instantly via your banking app if it goes
  missing, and consider using digital wallets on your phone since they require biometric locks (Face ID/fingerprint) to even
  open.
