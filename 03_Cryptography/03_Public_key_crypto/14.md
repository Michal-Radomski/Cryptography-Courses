That is correct! Traditional credit cards with **magnetic stripes are significantly less secure** than modern chip cards or
contactless payments.

Here is why magnetic stripes are vulnerable:

- **Static Data:** The information stored on a magnetic stripe—your card number, name, and expiration date—is completely
  static. It never changes, no matter how many times you use it.
- **Easy to Copy (Skimming):** Because the data is just written statically onto the magnetic track, anyone with a cheap card
  reader (a "skimmer") can easily copy all that information in a fraction of a second.
- **No Cryptography:** Unlike chip cards that use public-key cryptography to create a brand-new, unique digital signature for
  every single transaction, a magnetic stripe card just hands over the exact same piece of raw data every time. If a
  fraudster steals that data once, they can clone a fake card and use it anywhere.

This is why most of the world has transitioned away from magnetic stripes to **EMV chips** and contactless technology, where
a cloned card is useless because the dynamic security code changes with every purchase.
