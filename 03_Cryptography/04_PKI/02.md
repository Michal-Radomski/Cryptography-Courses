## What is a Public Key Certificate?

A **Public Key Certificate** (often just called a digital certificate) is an electronic document used to prove the ownership
of a public key.

Think of it as the digital equivalent of a passport or a driver's license. Just like a government issues a passport to verify
who you are, a trusted organization called a **Certificate Authority (CA)** issues a digital certificate to verify that a
specific public key truly belongs to a specific website, person, or device.

---

## What Information is Inside a Certificate?

A standard digital certificate (following the X.509 international standard) contains several key pieces of information:

- **Subject:** The name of the entity the certificate belongs to (for example, `example.com` or a user's name).
- **Public Key:** The actual public key belonging to that entity.
- **Issuer:** The name of the trusted Certificate Authority (CA) that created and signed the certificate.
- **Validity Period:** The start and expiration dates (certificates expire to ensure security keys are rotated and updated
  regularly).
- **Digital Signature:** A cryptographic signature created by the CA using _its own_ private key. This signature proves that
  the certificate hasn't been tampered with and really came from that trusted CA.

---

## How It Works in Everyday Browsing

When you type a secure website address into your browser, the website hands over its digital certificate. Your browser then
performs a quick background check:

1. **Check the Expiration:** Is the certificate still valid, or has it expired?
2. **Check the Issuer:** Does the browser trust the Certificate Authority that signed it? (Most operating systems and
   browsers come pre-loaded with a list of globally trusted CAs).
3. **Verify the Signature:** The browser uses the CA's public key to verify the CA's digital signature on the certificate.

If all these checks pass, your browser knows for sure that you are communicating with the real website and not an imposter,
allowing it to establish a secure, encrypted connection.
