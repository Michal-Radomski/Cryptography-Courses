DES (Data Encryption Standard) and AES (Advanced Encryption Standard) are both symmetric-key block ciphers used to encrypt
and protect digital information, but AES is the modern, highly secure successor to the outdated DES.

---

### DES (Data Encryption Standard)

- **History:** Developed by IBM in the early 1970s and adopted by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)
  in 1977 as an official federal standard.
- **Key Size:** 56 bits.
- **Block Size:** 64 bits.
- **Current Status:** Obsolete and insecure. Because computer processing power has grown exponentially, a 56-bit key can now
  be cracked via brute-force attacks in a matter of hours or minutes. It is no longer used for sensitive data security.

### AES (Advanced Encryption Standard)

- **History:** Selected by NIST in 2001 through a competitive process to replace DES after it became clear DES was no longer
  safe.
- **Key Size:** 128, 192, or 256 bits.
- **Block Size:** 128 bits.
- **Current Status:** The global gold standard for encryption. It is used worldwide to secure everything from HTTPS web
  traffic and Wi-Fi networks (WPA2/WPA3) to messaging apps and encrypted storage. To date, no practical attack has ever
  broken AES with a full key length.

---

### Quick Comparison

| Feature              | DES                                        | AES                                               |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Year Adopted**     | 1977                                       | 2001                                              |
| **Key Length**       | 56 bits                                    | 128, 192, or 256 bits                             |
| **Block Size**       | 64 bits                                    | 128 bits                                          |
| **Security Level**   | Insecure (easily cracked)                  | Highly secure (industry standard)                 |
| **Speed/Efficiency** | Fast for its time, but mathematically weak | Fast and optimized for both hardware and software |
